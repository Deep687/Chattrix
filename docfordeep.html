🔐 Product & Access Model

What Chattrix Is, and How Every Role Works

A companion to migration.plan.md and plan.html, written for one purpose: to state exactly what the product does, and exactly what each kind of user can and cannot do — including the boundary that makes the whole project interesting, which is that platform administrators cannot read tenant documents.

Tenancy unit: workspace = one company Role axes: 2, independent Enforcement: policy + query layer Ships in MVP: Owner / Member only
01

What the app is about

Chattrix is an internal policy & compliance assistant. A company uploads its own internal documents — HR handbook, IT & security policy, leave & benefits, code of conduct — and its employees ask plain-English questions, receiving answers that are grounded: every claim comes from the uploaded documents, each answer carries citations pointing back to the exact source passage, and when the answer genuinely isn't in the docs the app says "I don't know based on the provided documents" rather than guessing.

The unit of tenancy is the workspace: one workspace = one company. Every document, every chunk, and every retrieval query is scoped to a single workspace. That isn't a supporting feature — it is the product's headline claim:

Acme's leave policy can never appear in an answer given to a Globex employee.

Why isolation is the product, not a footnote

The portfolio market is full of single-user "chat with a PDF" demos. Pointing the same engine at company policy Q&A makes it read as a product — and makes tenant isolation the value proposition rather than a technical detail. It is also a genuine production concern almost no demo handles, which is exactly why it is worth proving with a test instead of claiming in a README.

02

A concrete journey

The clearest way to see the roles in motion is to follow two people through the app:

  1. Priya signs up. She belongs to no workspace yet, so she creates "Acme" — which makes her its Owner.

  2. She uploads acme-hr-handbook.pdf. It is parsed, cleaned, chunked into ~500-token passages with ~15% overlap, embedded via Gemini, and stored as document_chunks rows — each row stamped with workspace_id.

  3. She invites Rahul. He accepts and joins Acme as a Member.

  4. Rahul asks "How many casual leaves do I get?" The question is embedded; chunks are filtered to workspace_id = acme before the vector search; the top matches are ranked; a grounded prompt goes to Gemini.

  5. He gets "12 casual leaves per year" with a citation chip that reveals the exact handbook passage it came from.

  6. The same question asked inside Globex returns Globex's number. A Globex-only question asked inside Acme returns "I don't know" — and a named regression test asserts precisely that.

03

Two independent role axes

This is the part worth being precise about, because the app deliberately has two separate role systems that do not talk to each other.

Axis 1 — Platform role

users.role · App\Enums\UserRole

Who you are on Chattrix-the-service. Values: SuperAdmin | User. Global — one per account. Governs operating the service: billing, abuse, platform metrics.

Axis 2 — Tenant role

App\Enums\WorkspaceRole

What you can do inside one specific workspace. Values: Owner | Member. Per-workspace — the same person can be Owner of Acme and Member of Globex at the same time. Governs access to a company's documents.

Why keep them apart

They answer different questions, and separating them is what lets the project make its strongest claim: being a platform administrator grants zero document access. If the two axes were one, every admin feature would become a potential leak path.

04

Platform roles in detail

User default · ships in MVP

Every signup is this — users.role defaults to 'user'. The only platform role the MVP actually ships.

  • Create workspaces (becoming their Owner)
  • Be invited to workspaces and act with whatever tenant role they hold there
  • See anything about a workspace they are not a member of

SuperAdmin scaffolded · wired to nothing

The enum case and User::isSuperAdmin() exist in app/Models/User.php, but nothing calls them — deliberately. M1–M4 ship with no super-admin feature at all, because building it now would delay the demoable core. If it lands after M4, it is scoped to platform operations only:

  • List all workspaces
  • Cross-tenant usage, cost, and latency dashboards
  • View evaluation metrics
  • Disable an abusive tenant
  • Read any document
  • Read any chunk
  • Run a RAG query against a tenant
How the second list is enforced

Structurally, not by convention. The retrieval query filters on workspace_id with no super-admin bypass branch — there is no if ($user->isSuperAdmin()) return true; anywhere in the query path. Any break-glass access, if ever needed, goes through an explicit audited path, never a silent policy bypass. The README states it as a feature: "even platform admins cannot read tenant documents through the RAG path — isolation is in the query layer, not just the UI."

05

Tenant roles in detail

Owner ships in MVP

Creates the workspace. CreateWorkspaceAction sets workspaces.owner_id and attaches the creator to workspace_user — so an Owner is always also a member, which means a plain membership check already covers them. Exactly one per workspace today, since ownership is a single foreign key rather than a role row.

  • Update and delete the workspace
  • Upload and delete documents
  • Invite and remove members
  • Ask questions; view the member list

Member ships in MVP

Joins by invitation.

  • View the workspace and its documents
  • Upload documents
  • Ask questions; view the member list
  • Update or delete the workspace
  • Manage members or invites

Viewer deferred · not in M2

Could ask but not upload or delete — the natural next role once you want employees who consume policies without curating them. Worth naming in an interview as a known next step; not worth building yet.

07

Where enforcement lives — two layers, both required

LAYER 1

WorkspacePolicy — the HTTP gate

In app/Policies/WorkspacePolicy.php. Today: view, create, join, update, delete. The plan adds viewDocuments, uploadDocument, ask. Owner checks compare $workspace->owner_id to the user's id; member checks are an existence query against workspace_user.

LAYER 2

The retrieval query — the data gate

Every RagService lookup filters document_chunks.workspace_id before the vector search. workspace_id is denormalised onto document_chunks precisely so this filter needs no join and can never be quietly forgotten.

Why both

The policy protects the route; the query protects the data. A bug in one is caught by the other. M3 is the gate that proves it: a named feature test that uploads policy docs to both Acme and Globex, asks cross-tenant, and asserts no leak plus an "I don't know" response.

08

Decisions locked for the access model

  • Private workspaces only. The privacy_type enum is dropped — a public workspace is one where isolation is deliberately off, which contradicts the product's headline claim and would put an unproven branch inside the policy that guards retrieval.
  • Access rule is one sentence: you can see a workspace if and only if you have a workspace_user row for it.
  • Joining is invite-only via hashed, expiring, single-use invite tokens — the same token discipline already used for refresh_token (hash at rest, expires_at, revoked_at), so invites extend the existing security story instead of introducing an unrelated concept.
  • Email-domain auto-join is deliberately not built. An unverified @acme.com address is a self-asserted claim, and free providers would collapse every consumer signup into one workspace. It needs DNS TXT domain verification and a public-suffix blocklist first — worth saying in an interview, not worth shipping now.
  • Guest, public, and anonymous access are out of scope. A recruiter-facing demo is solved with a pre-joined demo account, not by weakening the isolation invariant.
09

Gaps between this model and the code — now closed

Three places where the code had not caught up to the model above. All three are fixed, and each is now held in place by a test in tests/Feature/Workspace/WorkspaceAccessTest.php.

1. WorkspaceRole was dead code fixed

The enum existed with Owner/Member, but workspace_user had only user_id, workspace_id, and joined_at — no role column — so the enum was referenced nowhere and tenant role was implicitly derived from workspaces.owner_id.

Fixed by adding a role column to the pivot, defaulting to member. CreateWorkspaceAction writes owner, JoinWorkspaceAction takes a WorkspaceRole parameter, and Workspace::hasMemberWithRole() reads it. The column was added rather than the enum deleted because Viewer needs it, and because it is where an invite writes its baked-in role.

2. The policy branched on privacy_type fixed

view() returned true for any public workspace, and join() permitted only public ones.

Fixed by removing privacy_type from the migration, model $fillable, WorkspaceResource, and both form requests. view() is now hasMember(). The self-serve join route, controller method, and policy ability are gone — JoinWorkspaceAction survives as the membership primitive that invite acceptance will call.

3. The listing returned every workspace fixed

viewAny() returned true unconditionally and index called WorkspaceService::paginate(), which paginated the whole workspaces table — disclosing which companies exist on the instance.

Fixed by pointing index at fetchMyWorkspaces() and deleting paginate(). The now-duplicate /workspaces/me route was removed in favour of the scoped index.
